Three independent switches

In the demo, open Personality & little tricks. Allow expressive actions is the master switch. Gentle idle antics permits only a curious glance or wink after 45 seconds without activity, then at most once a minute. Quiet listening nods / head shakes accepts occasional verified, silent decision-model cues (at least 10 seconds apart). All are on initially and remembered locally. The listening feature piggybacks on existing enabled live usefulness/rule decisions; it never creates a second model loop.

Idle tricks stop when speech or a new request arrives. A listening nod means “I am following”, not factual agreement. Head shakes require a clear reason; they are not random disagreement. Large turns, eye rolling and grimaces are never selected as idle antics.

Playback, comfort and limits

Spoken-response actions begin at the audio playback position, not when a network message arrives. Sentence offsets are estimates from the acting script, not forced alignment. A barge-in, Stop button, connection close, hidden tab or eye-contact calibration clears queued actions. Camera tracking is preserved beneath the temporary animation. “Stop animation” only stops visuals, not speech.

System “reduced motion” disables spins, nod/shake motion and idle antics; facial poses remain available. The desk page renders a real virtual 3-D yaw turn; the older SVG avatar uses a flat cartoon turn. Explicit negative emotions can look angry or sad; only the playful grimace presets are constrained to a warm, non-threatening style. Cuteness is a design intention, not a perceptual guarantee.

Screen simulation only. A 360° trick deliberately exceeds the prototype’s physical ±90° pan limits. No motor commands are sent. Do not transfer these movements to hardware without mechanical and cable-clearance checks. Short spoken “Stop” still has known reliability limits; the visible Stop reply button remains available.

For developers

The tool is perform_avatar. Its JSON is validated in Python, sent separately from spoken text, checked again in JavaScript, and rendered locally. No new ML model, camera processing, persistent identity data or GPU memory is added. Dialogue actions currently use a brief spoken acknowledgment to anchor playback; silent actions are available through the preview controls and the listening-decision path.
