## 1. The general-quality adapter ships at weight 1.5 — decided by ear, and recorded as such

`laion/moss-va-sft3-quality-dpo-lora`, checkpoint 1504, **merge weight 1.5**.

This is a *listening* decision, and the honest reason is that the statistics could not make it.
Eleven weights over 220 takes, and on genuineness **no weight reached significance**:

| weight | 1.00 | 1.25 | 1.50 | 1.75 | 2.50 | 3.00 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Δ genuineness | +0.42 | +0.01 | **+0.48** | +0.58 | +0.47 | +0.52 |
| Δ word error | +0.016 | −0.013 | **+0.006** | +0.145 | +0.148 | +0.039 |

The one statistically solid result in the entire sweep is a *harm* — word error +0.148 at 2.5.
Between 1.0, 1.5 and 1.75 the test is simply blind: 20 sentences per level cannot separate +0.48
from +0.58. So the choice was made by listening, on
[the sweep page](https://huggingface.co/spaces/laion/moss-quality-adapter-listening), and 1.5 is
what it returned. **1.5 also happens to be where the word-error cost is still nil** — the jump to
+0.145 lands at 1.75, one step further.

This is the second time on this adapter that an ear has decided something a table could not:
a DNSMOS ranking put checkpoint 1504 last of ten, and a listener picked it as the best. Both are
recorded, because the pattern matters more than either result.

`coefficients.json → global.quality_dpo` carries this, so the server reads it rather than a
number typed into a config file.

## 2. Steering: the measurement and the listener are both right, and they are not about the same thing

`REVISION_2026-08-31.md §1` says: do not act on `steer`. That still holds as a *shipping*
decision. But the reason has to be stated precisely, because two separate findings have been
collapsed into one in at least one published page.

**What the measurements say.** The 12,136-clip steering study finds that the delivery-style axes
steer strongly and *for free*: `S_RANT` +3.95 (t 8.75), `S_WHIS` +1.85 with word error
*improving* by 0.065, `S_DRAM` +1.78 (t 8.58) with word error improving by 0.044. Emotion, at its
best free setting, moves +0.10. That ordering tracks decodability exactly — W1 measured R² 0.83
for `S_RANT` against 0.47 for the emotion heads. **You can steer what the model represents well.**

**What the listener says.** With steering active in the demo server the result was *more
emotional, with strange artefacts, and the timbre off*. The server was running `STEER_ALPHA = 0.1`
with a ceiling of `0.15` — that is, **inside** the range the measurements call good. So the
listener's report is not a far-tail artefact and does not go away by lowering α.

**The two are compatible, and the gap between them is a specific untested cell.** Every number in
the steering study is one model scoring another model's output; no listening test has ever been
run on any of it. And the listener's session used the *emotion* axes — the family where the
measured effect is +0.10, i.e. nearly nothing, so almost anything audible there is artefact. The
open question is narrow: **does `S_RANT` / `S_DRAM` / `S_WHIS` steering at α ≈ 0.1 sound right?**
Nobody has listened. Until somebody does, `GEN_MODE=adapter` stays.

**A correction to our own write-up.** The layer-forensics report stated that steering "destroys
the output at every strength tested" and that a random direction does the same damage. That was
true of the grid it described — which started at α = 0.5 — and it is misleading now that α ≤ 0.3
has been measured. The published page has been corrected; `CITED.alpha_break` in the generator
already carried 0.30 and did not need changing.

## 3. Vocal-burst hit rates: the six-class picture was not the seventy-class picture

`REVISION_2026-08-31.md §4` reported three of six tested classes never realising. That
generalisation was drawn from six classes. Round 2 measured all seventy at seven weights, and the
shape of the problem is worse than six classes suggested: **of 140 class × placement cells, only
37 show a non-zero hit rate at any weight.** The rest never produce the requested burst.

Round 2's per-cell sample was 10 prompts, which is too thin to choose a weight — the standard
error on a hit rate near 0.2 is about 0.13, so 0.17 and 0.27 are the same number. A round-3
measurement is running as this is written: the 64 class adapters that have never been given a
recommendation, the same 10 carrier prompts (so the two rounds pool), six weights, both
placements, and **nine samples per prompt instead of three** — 90 generations per cell, standard
error about 0.05. This section will be replaced with the per-class table when it lands.

### The metric was changed before the numbers arrived, by decision, and it is written down here

**The family-relaxed hit rate is now the primary metric for a burst adapter; the strict rate stays
as the secondary.** Decided by the project owner on 2026-09-02 — *"die entspannten Klassengrenzen
sind vollkommen in Ordnung"* — while round 3 was still generating, so this is a pre-registration
and not a metric picked after seeing a result. A director who asks for a chuckle and gets a giggle
has, for this project's purposes, been served; the detector's label vocabulary is finer than the
distinction anyone is making.

That decision made the family table load-bearing, and it was not fit for it. Measured: **33 of the
64 classes mapped to no family at all**, so their "relaxed" rate was silently the strict rate.
Two things were wrong and both are fixed in `vb_filter/vf_compare.py`:

* **A tokenisation bug, not a judgement about sounds.** The matcher wanted an exact token, so
  `coughing` did not match `cough`, `humming` did not match `hum`, `panting` did not match `pant`,
  `breathing` did not match `breath`, `gulps` did not match `gulp`. Light stemming, including the
  doubled-consonant case (`humming` -> `humm` -> `hum`), fixes it.
* **Three families that never existed:** `sob` (sob, cry, weep), `whistle`, and `mouth` (smack,
  lip, lick, kiss, chew, slurp, suck, drink, swallow, gulp, spit, tongue, click, tsk, hiccup).
  `growl` and `snarl` were added to `groan`.

Coverage went from 31 to **62 of 64**. `gurgling` and `hiss` are deliberately left unmapped:
neither has a neighbour in this set, and a family of one would dress the strict rate up as a
relaxed one. If `sa_compare.md` is ever regenerated its relaxed column will move — that is this
change, not new data.

The one adapter that gets no per-class hit rate is `blend_genuine_emotional`: it has no single
target burst, so "was the requested burst produced" is undefined for it. It is measured on blend
and genuineness instead.

## 4. The emotion-adapter "contrast compression" was measured on the wrong yardstick

`REVISION_2026-08-31.md §3` reports that the emotion adapters compress the contrast they are
meant to create — endpoint distance 4.587 without the adapter against 1.982 with it, factor 2.3,
same direction in all twelve readings — and concludes: do not build an emotional turn out of
emotion adapters.

**The measurement is right and the conclusion does not follow.** Pointed out by the project owner,
2026-09-02: for a *transition* the goal is not the largest possible distance between the first and
the last frame. It is that the path between them is smooth and organic, frame by frame across the
80 ms vectors. Endpoint contrast does not measure that. A trajectory that snaps between two states
scores perfectly on contrast and is exactly the thing a transition should not do.

That also re-reads the other transition result. The two-take splice (`STEP`) won on the same
yardstick — but a splice is *by construction* the maximally discontinuous answer: two independent
takes butted together. Winning a contrast contest is what it should do; it says nothing about
whether the seam is audible.

**So the status of both is `open`, not `negative`.** Neither the adapters nor the splice has been
measured on continuity, because **no continuity metric exists in this project yet**. Candidates
that would need no new generation, only re-scoring the clips already on disk: per-frame emotion
trajectory monotonicity, the size of the largest single-frame jump, and whether the trajectory
stays inside the convex hull of the two endpoints.

`hf.co/spaces/laion/emotion-continuity-20` was built for exactly this gap and already says so on
its own front page — "the question here is continuity, not contrast; the scorer answers the first
question and cannot answer the second". 360 clips, 20 transitions × 3 sentence pairs × 6
conditions, with a text feedback field. **Nobody has listened to it yet**, and until somebody does
there is no evidence either way about which method actually flows.

## 6. The rule this revision earns

**Name the yardstick, not just the result.** Item 4 is the sharpest case: a correct number
("contrast falls by 2.3x") carried a conclusion it does not support, because nobody wrote down
that contrast is not what a transition is for. A finding should state the quantity it measured
before it states what should be done about it.

**Say which of the two evidence kinds a recommendation rests on.** Three times now a scored
measurement and a human ear have disagreed on this project — the DNSMOS ranking of checkpoint
1504, the steering artefacts at α = 0.1, and the choice of 1.5 over 1.0. In every case both were
correct about what they measured. A recommendation in this wiki should therefore say whether it
came from a scorer or from a listener, and `coefficients.json` now carries `chosen_by` for the
first entry where the distinction decided the value.
