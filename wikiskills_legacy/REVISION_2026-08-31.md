# Revision — 31 August 2026

`index.md`, `interactions.md`, `coefficients.json` and every page under `patterns/` were generated
on 2026-08-29 from measurements that were correct as computed. Four things have happened since
that change what should be *acted on*. This file overrides the pages where they conflict; the
pages are left intact because the underlying numbers are still the record of what was measured.

---

## 1. Steering is superseded. Do not act on `steer` in any recommendation.

The index recommends a mode containing `steer` for most of the 40 emotions, often as the balanced
operating point. **Those recommendations should not be followed.**

A listener using the demo server reported that with steering active the result was *more
emotional, with strange artefacts, and the timbre off*. The shipped default is now
`GEN_MODE=adapter`. Nothing in the steering study is retracted — it measured what it measured —
but every figure in it is one model scoring another model's output, and no listening test was ever
run on any of its results. `docs/LEVERS.md` in the demo server says so explicitly.

**What to do instead:** read the `adapter` and `cfg` columns. Where an attribute's only usable
point involved steering, treat it as **none** — the honest answer is that we do not have a
measured lever for it, not that steering is available.

## 2. Guidance: usable to g = 4.0, and now with a second, independent measurement behind it

The field report said guidance is fine to about 4.0 and degenerates above. The crossfade study
(protocol §40) then measured guidance from a completely different direction and agreed:

| g | 0 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| anchor separation (SD) | 0.797 | 1.013 | 1.934 | **2.239** |
| texts ≥ 1 SD (of 30) | 11 | 14 | 18 | **23** |

The rise is monotone on every contrast × segmentation × anchor set, with **no reversal anywhere in
the grid**. Guidance does not merely change the emotion score — it makes two intended emotions
*more distinguishable from each other*.

`CFG_G_MAX` is raised 3.0 → 4.0 (PR #6). The family defaults `CFG_G = {emo 3.0, vn 2.5, qual 2.5}`
are unchanged: the evidence says 4.0 is the top of the usable range, not that the defaults are too
low. **Cost: 1.90–1.94× wall clock**, measured three times now, and identical at g = 2, 3 and 4 —
the price is the second branch, not the strength. Guidance cannot stream.

## 3. Emotion adapters *compress* the contrast they are meant to create

Measured in the crossfade study, and it is the least expected result of the week. Comparing the
emotional distance between two intended endpoints:

* without the emotion adapter: **4.587**
* with it: **1.982**

A factor of 2.3, in the same direction in all twelve readings at g = 3 and g = 4. The
within-condition scatter is flat (2.16–3.13), so this is the numerator shrinking, not the noise
growing.

**Consequence for transitions:** do not build an emotional turn by crossfading two emotion
adapters. The two-take splice (`STEP`) — generate each half separately and concatenate — beat every
adapter-side and prompt-side arm, and was **the only arm to beat a shuffled control at all four
guidance levels**. The best non-STEP cell anywhere sat *below* the noise floor. Ship `STEP` at
g = 3.

This does not mean the emotion adapters are useless — they are still the measured lever for a
single sustained emotion. It means they are the wrong tool for a *change* of emotion inside one
utterance.

## 4. Vocal-burst dose: the shipped values are correct, and the real problem is elsewhere

`BURST_LAM = 0.25` (inline) and `BURST_LAM_INTENSE = 0.5` (standalone) were conservative guesses
carried over from an older seven-adapter set and had never been swept. They have now been swept —
ten weights, under the real default stack — and **both should stay** (protocol §43).

In the shipping arm no weight beats the shipped one by a detectable margin on the pre-registered
metric, and for standalone bursts the shipped 0.5 is second-largest of nine measured values.
Turning the adapter *off* is significantly worse.

**But the study's own primary metric was the wrong one, and it says so.** `r_burst_cls` is an F1
that pays only 0.35 for a wrong-class burst and multiplies by `exp(-|dur err|/0.5)`, so *more*
bursts and *longer* bursts both push it down — which is precisely what raising the dose does
(+30 % detections, +24 % duration). On plain **hit-rate**, over the same 7,260 takes, the dose
does move: **+0.061 (t 2.32) at w = 0.5 rising to +0.091 (t 2.98) at w = 1.5** in `stack/solo`.

So the honest statement is narrower than "the dose does not matter": *no weight is preferable on
the metric we fixed in advance, while a plainer counting metric prefers a higher one, and its
maximum sits at w = 1.5 — the top of the swept range, which is not an optimum.* A forced-choice
listening test on `stack/solo` at 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 would settle it; those clips already exist.
Until then **0.25 / 0.5 stay**, because that is what the pre-registered rule returns and changing
a shipped default on a post-hoc metric is how noise becomes doctrine.

**The finding that matters more:** three of six tested burst classes essentially never realise at
any dose. `frustrated_groan` produced **0 correct bursts in 1,188 prompted cues**, `shriek` 11 in
1,045 — both arms, every weight, both script kinds, with every cell generating cleanly. The
failures are never silence: the model substitutes reliably **down the arousal axis** (frustrated
groan → contented sigh, 127 times). **No merge weight lifts a floor of zero.** If a director asks
for a shriek or a groan today, expect not to get one.

One concrete lead, worth checking before retraining anything: `frustrated_groan`'s labels came
from a sidecar rescan that never entered the corpus column, so it is worth confirming that its
adapter was trained on what its name says.

## 5. Never merge an adapter into the weights

Applies to all 17 LAION MOSS adapter repositories. `audio_lm_heads.0 … 11` are **weight-tied** to
`audio_embeddings.N` — one allocation, two names. A merge writes the head delta into the embedding
table and destroys generation irrecoverably; it does not fail loudly. Load with PEFT, set
`module.scaling[name]`, leave unmerged. Arithmetically identical, so nothing about the sound
changes. Verify with `head.weight.data_ptr() == embedding.weight.data_ptr()`.

## Three method rules earned this week

These are not about the model; they are about how to read the tables in this wiki.

**Measure the configuration you ship.** The two quality adapters measured as a flat null on bare
SFT3 and as a real effect inside the full demo-server stack — same adapters, same day. An adapter
behaves differently inside a five-adapter stack than alone. Every recommendation here should name
the stack it was measured in.

**A null on the wrong metric is not a null on the phenomenon.** A DNSMOS table ranked `qual1504`
last of ten states; a listener then picked it as the best. The signal that was there and got
under-weighted: it had the highest genuineness of any condition. DNSMOS was the right metric for
*selecting training data* and the wrong one for *judging a performance*.

**A best value at the edge of a swept range is not an optimum.** It cannot be distinguished from
the last point before one. Extend the range instead of reporting it.
