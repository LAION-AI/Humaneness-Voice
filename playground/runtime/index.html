1Generation & beam searchSingle take, Best-of-N or continuous chunk search

Live search / Beam Search

800 ms: more frequent selection and scoring. 1600 ms: less frequent selection, usually higher throughput. BS is the number of parallel candidates per search step, not the number of completed takes.

Top-2 can stream only a shared audio prefix. If candidates diverge, playback may wait until the end. BS 1 uses one path. The search limit is a maximum duration. Speech boundaries and fades buffer about 1 second by default: Parakeet word timestamps control live fades and safe cutoff; the completed take receives forced alignment.

Complete takes / Best-of-N

Single take generates one audio. Best-of-N scores complete takes and highlights the best; All N retains every take. Best-of-N selection requires completed takes, so it is not live-streamed.

Streaming PCM decodes incrementally. A single take streams live with buffered fades; Best-of-N waits for final scoring. Winner search and beam search can stream committed audio.

Does the reference stay the same?

Yes, when dynamic selection is off. Each catalog voice has a fixed reference. Emotion, language and direction do not change it. Dynamic VoiceCLAP selection is an explicit opt-in. The optional identity reward uses the actual reference. Uploaded references remain until removed.

4Sampling & playbackTemperature, CFG, seed, duration and fades

Temperature controls randomness; 0 uses argmax sampling. CFG amplifies the difference between acting directions and the neutral comparison; higher is not always better. Use the same settings, reference and seed for comparisons.

Speech boundaries and fades are on by default: Parakeet word timestamps for buffered live audio, Qwen forced alignment for completed takes. Cutoff requires the complete target text; requested pauses and bursts are protected. Buffering adds startup latency. Autoplay also controls live playback. Output: mono MP3, 96 kbps.

Loading available dimensions from the server…

VoiceCLAP compares audio with delivery descriptions. Luna adaptive dimensions selects targets when you send; preview makes them editable first. Manual allows 1–4 dimensions, targets and weights from VoiceNet and Empathic Insight Plus.

Speaker vector & limitations

VoiceNet uses 15 fixed-scale attributes: voice age, perceived gender, register, chest/head/oral/nasal/throat/mask resonance, fullness, brightness, warmth, roughness, metallic character and harmonicity. Values are centered on −1…1 before cosine similarity. Emotion and recording conditions affect these attributes; they are not a unique speaker fingerprint. Alternatively use ECAPA 192-D speaker embeddings. Live search scores the latest 800/1600-ms chunk; complete takes use the whole audio. Short chunks are less reliable; burst checks remain a separate optional term.

6Sounds / burst checksOptional: is the sound in the right place?

A good Burst Blend score does not prove that a requested laugh or sigh occurred. This additional check locates events and compares position, duration and optionally type. Off by default; can be combined with adaptive rewards.

Metrics & speedRTF below 1 means faster than real time

Single take / Best-of-N: on-device RTF = TTS/codec time divided by total generated audio duration, aggregated across takes. Search RTF also includes ASR, rewards and selection. Neither includes network, MP3 encoding or playback; neither promises real-time browser delivery.

Burst reward: explanation & technical validation (English) ↗

Optional addition to VoiceCLAP or adaptive dimensions: Locator v2 locates actual burst windows; the Commercial Detector classifies only those windows. Supports 16 types, but is not definitive proof. Unknown types receive position-only checks. Additional encoding/scoring cost; earlier RTF measurements do not apply.

Default: 25% burst score + 75% previous quality, then × WER gate; weight is adjustable. Each event can match one target. Future bursts are not penalized as missing during search; all targets count at the end. Without explicit targets the reward is unchanged. Use GENERAL/SCRIPT for automatic positions, or manual targets for CAPTION. Word-relative matching tolerates changes in speaking pace.
